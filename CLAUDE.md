# Desert — vehicular destruction game

Original Carmageddon-style car-smashing game (the player drives the silver car: `GAME_CONFIG.playerColor`) in a low-poly desert (orange dunes, rock walls, blue sky). three.js + Rapier physics + TypeScript, built with Vite, deployed to GitHub Pages.

Camera: a **chase view behind the car is the default**. **C** cycles chase → first-person cockpit → the original fixed 3/4 overhead view (which never rotates), and the choice is remembered in `localStorage['desert.camera.v2']`. It follows the car from a distance, so cars can stay simple and low-poly. The star feature is **visible damage**: car bodies dent per vertex and parts (bumpers, hood, doors, wheels) break off as physics debris.

Plus a real-world street circuit: **Chicago Loop**, generated offline from OpenStreetMap data.

Roadmap: M1 sandbox (driving + destruction) → M2 time trial on a ~7 km circuit → M3 races against 5 AI rivals → **M4 Destruction Bowl derby (a level for working on collisions)** + **Crossover** (walled asphalt figure 8) → dash ability → roguelike runs (upgrades as stat modifiers). The user dislikes horde games and wants purpose; Atari *Combat*-style battles and destruction-derby racing are welcome directions.

## Commands
- `npm run dev`: local dev server
- `npm run build`: typecheck + production build to `dist/` (relative base `./`, works under any Pages path)
- `npm run preview`: serve the build
- `npm run check`: `tsc --noEmit` (TypeScript 7)
- `npm test`: Vitest (node environment, `src/**/*.test.ts`). Tests' `console.log` only shows with `--silent=false`.
- `npm run osm:chicago`: rebuild `src/city/chicago*.json` from the cached Overpass download (`-- --fetch` downloads again; the cache in `scripts/.osm-cache/` is git-ignored)

Deploy: push to `main`. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` tests, builds and deploys. In the repo settings, Pages source must be "GitHub Actions".

### Key designs
- **Axes:** car forward is +Z, up is +Y, left is +X. The vehicle controller uses `setIndexForwardAxis = 2`, and the wheel axle is (−1,0,0).
- **Impact detection:** damage is driven by the chassis' *sudden velocity change per physics step* (minus gravity), `dv > stats.dentThreshold` (m/s). Contact-force events only record *who* we touched that step. This means steady contact (lying on the roof, scraping a wall) never dents, and both cars in a crash take damage. `world.contactPair()` then supplies the chassis-local contact points and normal. If there's no manifold, the point is inferred from the dv direction.
- **Crash balance (`Car.hitScale`, `Crash.ts`):** the jolt is scaled by what was hit before the threshold applies. Static world (terrain, rocks, posts) × `worldDamage` 0.4; other dynamic things × 1; another car × `carDamage` 1.3 × `ramFactor`. `ramFactor` compares how fast each car was driving into the other (pre-step velocities along the centre line, weighed by speed squared): even = 1, a car ramming a parked one gets 1 − `ramAdvantage` (0.7) and the victim 1 + 0.7. Mass is already in the jolt (the heavier car is shoved less) and `toughness` divides on top. **Car hits take only `carHealthLoss` (0.75) of their damage off health** (the user found cars wrecked too fast: it takes a third more hits); dents, lost parts, knocks and spins are unchanged, and so are wall crashes (`Car.hit`'s `wear`). Measured at 100 km/h: wall 65 hp of 450 (was 182; `maxHealth` was 300 then); T-bone rammer 19 vs victim 159 (25/210 before `carHealthLoss`); head-on against a car at half speed 84 vs 218; even head-on at 60 km/h vs Scorpion-like heavy 136 vs 64.
- **Knock:** a hit's horizontal jolt (× the same scale) above `knockThreshold` 3 m/s knocks the car for up to `knockTime` 1 s (full at `knockFull` 12): grip × down to `knockGrip` 0.15, yaw control × (1 − knock), slide cap lifted. The arcade grip/yaw model otherwise cancels a crash's spin within ~0.3 s; with the knock, Rapier's own response carries. A sideways tail hit at 60 km/h spins the victim ~100°, at 100 km/h ~180°, while the rammer keeps control. `Car.knock` (0..1) is public.
- **Hit spin** (`Car.spinFromHit`): an off-centre car hit adds a yaw kick = `hitSpin` 2.5 × scale × (contact point × jolt leverage) / halfLength², capped at `hitSpinMax` 4 rad/s, and knocks the car in proportion. Without it a matched-speed nudge on the rear quarter (2–4 m/s) turned the victim only ~20°; with it an AI spin/shove attack turns it 95–140° and puts it 55–65 m off the line.
- **Repair** (`Car.repair(dt)`, stats `repairTime` 20, `repairTau` 7, `repairRefit` 2): health, part HP and every dented vertex ease back to the originals along `repairStep` (each vertex by its own depth, so seam duplicates stay together); lost parts are refitted one per 2 s of continuous repair (wheels first) as fresh clones of pristine spares kept per part (Debris owns and disposes the detached mesh). A 0.15 s gap restarts the refit wait. A wrecked car under repair drives again at 20% health.
- **Soft CCD** (`setSoftCcdPrediction(1.5)` on the chassis): without it, two cars closing at ≥ ~56 m/s overlapped deeper than the 0.64 m-tall chassis box in one step, were pushed apart vertically and passed through each other.
- **Deformation:** the chassis collider is a fixed cuboid; only the visuals deform. Vertices near the point are pushed along −normal with a smoothstep falloff. Cumulative displacement from the original shape is clamped to `maxDent`. A smooth "crumple" field (~0.5 m wavelength, a function of vertex *position*) varies the depth, so neighbouring vertices fold together instead of spiking, and duplicate seam vertices move together so meshes never tear. Per-vertex random noise looked spiky, so don't use it. Flat shading (`flatShading: true`) means no normal recompute is needed.
- **Parts:** each detachable part has HP. At 0 it is re-parented into a debris rigid body (capped pool, oldest removed first). **Wheels** (120 HP) take `wheelDamage` 3.5 × hit strength × nearness: a direct side hit at the axle costs ~41 HP at 50 km/h, ~62 at 70, ~90 at 100, so no single hit takes a wheel off and two big ones do (it used to be ×6 against 90 HP: one 70 km/h hit, and a 100 km/h nose hit took both front wheels). **Steering needs the front wheels** (`Car.steerable`): each lost front wheel halves the steer input, handbrake turns included; with both gone the car only goes straight. `maxHealth` is 450 (was 300) for every car. A lost wheel has its radius and friction zeroed in the vehicle controller, since Rapier can't remove wheels. The chassis friction then drops to `rimFriction` (0.12, Min combine rule) so the bare corner scrapes along: with the normal 0.5 (averaged with the ground's 0.9) a car that lost a wheel or two was anchored to the spot. A refit of the last lost wheel restores 0.5/Average.
- **Handling: arcade drift model (`Car.applyDrive` / `Car.applyGrip`).** Rapier's vehicle controller only does suspension and ground contact: its tyre friction is set to 0. We tried driving through Rapier's tyres and abandoned it: its tyres share one grip budget between engine and cornering, so throttle broke traction, and cornering scrubbed speed. The patches stacked on top (rear-grip bias, yaw assist, countersteer, damping) made it worse.
  - **Drive:** thrust (`engineForce`, N) acts along the nose at the centre of mass, scaled by grounded wheels. It never consumes grip, so throttle can't break traction, and mid-drift it powers the car through. Brakes, rolling and handbrake are decelerations in m/s².
  - **Grip:** rotates the planar velocity toward the nose at most `gripAccel / speed` rad/s, *keeping its magnitude*, minus `driftDrag × |sin slip|`. Faster means a slower-bending path, so **drifts come from cornering speed**. Sand reduces grip by `sandSlip × softness`.
  - **Yaw:** body rotation follows the bicycle-model rate `v·tan(δ)/wheelbase`, approached at `yawResponse` and capped by `maxYawRate`. The slide angle is held at `driftMaxSlip` by clamping the target to the path's turn rate.
  - **Steering lock** is `maxSteer / (1 + v / steerFadeSpeed)`. Gentle steering stays within grip at any speed, while full lock exceeds it more the faster you go.
  - **Reverse:** the lock is × `reverseSteer` (1.3) while rolling backwards (full lock: 5.8 m radius at 22 km/h, 8.4 m at 43), and `reverseForce` is 7500 N (0–60 km/h backwards in ~3 s), capped at 35% of `topSpeed`. The derby AI's reverse pursuit uses the same multiplier (`SteeringModel.reverseSteer`).
  - **Centred wheel:** the yaw target is `-slip × selfAlign` (default 0). It must NOT include `pathRate`: nose and path would chase each other and the car kept turning on its own.
  - **Handbrake (Space):** grip × `handbrakeGrip`, recovering at `gripRecovery`/s after release. The yaw target becomes `steer × handbrakeYawRate`, independent of the speed-reduced steering lock. The slide cap is lifted.
  - **Spin rate and direction:** the handbrake spin rate is scaled by speed over the ground (`sp`), NOT forward speed along the nose. Forward speed falls to 0 as the car turns sideways, which used to stall handbrake turns at ~90° (and reverse them past it). The spin direction (`spinDir`) is latched when the handbrake goes on.
  - **Committed spin:** releasing the handbrake with the car slid ≥ `spinCommit` (0.87 rad ≈ 50°, reached by a ~0.4 s press) carries the rotation on to `spinEnd` (170°): a one-press 180. Countersteer, re-applying the handbrake, or dropping below 2 m/s ends it. Shorter flicks are unchanged and settle into a normal drift (slide cap `driftMaxSlip`).
  - **No donuts:** a handbrake spin (held or committed) stops forcing rotation once the car has turned `spinMaxTurn` (3.0 rad) since the press, landing at ~181° after the spin winds down. While handbraking, engine push is × `handbrakeThrust` (0.25: locked rear wheels don't drive). Handbraking or spinning also scrubs `spinScrub` (7 m/s²) × |sin slide angle|. Before this, holding handbrake + 80% throttle orbited ~4 turns in 8 s.
  - **Measured from 72 km/h:** plain steering turns ~26°; a 0.2 s tap ~48° keeping speed; a 0.3 s flick gives a drift that straightens out; a 0.4–0.5 s press gives about 160–210° (at 60–80 km/h), ending facing back; at 120+ km/h a slightly longer press is needed to commit. Holding with throttle gives ~181° at any speed, then the car slides on backwards, slowing (72 km/h → ~40 right after the spin).
  - **Spin hangs past sideways:** past `spinLateFrom` (90° of slide) the spin rate eases over ~25° down to `spinLateRate` (0.45) × `handbrakeYawRate`, so a held spin takes 0.5 s to reach 90° and another ~0.65 s to finish the 180 (it used to whip from 90° to 194° in 0.35 s). Throttle no longer brakes while handbraking or spinning: past 90° the car counts as rolling backwards, and throttle-as-brake used to stop held spins dead (60 → 8 km/h in 0.4 s).
  - **Measured cornering:**
    - Half steer grips at 54 and 90 km/h, and drifts 12–16° at 126 km/h. Full lock drifts ~27–31° at 90–126 km/h.
    - Throttle keeps or gains speed through corners; lifting off loses ~25%.
    - At a steady speed, throttle adds no slide.
  - `Car.test.ts` "cornering" and "handbrake" lock all of this in.
- **Other assists:** speed-squared downforce, air control, auto-right when stuck upside down or lying steeply tilted (up.y < 0.7) on fewer than 3 wheels (keeps damage), and R for a full reset: `Game.spawnPlayer()` gives a fresh, repaired car at the current spot and heading. 0–100 km/h in 2.75 s (`engineForce` 13200), and ~335 km/h after 25 s.
- **Heightfield internal edges:** the terrain collider uses `HeightFieldFlags.FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES`. Without it, a hard landing that bottoms the suspension lets the chassis box snag a triangle edge: 260 → 12 km/h in one step plus a spin, with no damage. Keep the flag on any heightfield or trimesh cars drive on.
- **Heightfield gotcha (verified by raycast test):** Rapier heightfields are column-major, `heights[ix * n + iz]`, built with `heightfield(n-1, n-1, …)`. `TerrainData` stores row-major `iz * n + ix` and converts. Rapier splits each cell along the `(ix, iz+1)`–`(ix+1, iz)` diagonal. `sampleHeight` and the visual mesh use the same triangles, so physics, visuals and sampling agree.
- **Desert terrain:**
  - Asymmetric transverse dunes run across a wind direction (+X rotated 20°): a gentle windward slope, a steep lee face, and flat interdune troughs. They are domain-warped, with ~50–85 m spacing.
  - The arena (radius 40) has ~2 m rolls. Dunes grow to ~14 m by 120 m out, plus hills and ramps.
  - `softness` (0 firm .. 1 deep) is high on dune bodies and lee faces, and low in the troughs and the arena (~0.15).
  - `Car.surface` (a `SurfaceFn`, set by Game to use `terrain.softnessAt` for the terrain collider and 0 for everything else) drives `applySandDrag`: horizontal drag of `sandDrag × softness`. Measured: top speed 233 → 173 km/h on deep sand, and coasting bleeds much faster. Soft sand also sprays more dust.
  - Firm ground is coloured a slightly paler, dustier orange. Keep the palette saturated: an earlier beige hardpan washed out the look.
  - Parked cars hold `input.brake = 1` so they don't roll down dunes.
- **Race track (M2):**
  - World is 2048 m square (`GAME_CONFIG.terrainN` 769, ~2.7 m cells; ~0.4 s to generate). Keep tracks within ±800 m (the rim starts at ~880).

Being held up (braking for a slower car close ahead) adds `temper × blockedAnger` (1.5)/s; being overtaken within 25 m adds `pride`. `calm` (base 0.035/s) decays it, so a fight lasts ~10–25 s.

Spin/shove match the target's speed (`matchSpeed`): at full throttle they used to overshoot it.

- **Chicago Loop** (new): a 3-lap race against the 5 rivals on real downtown Chicago streets from OpenStreetMap: LaSalle, Jackson, and Wacker along the river (~1720 extruded buildings, barricaded side streets with street-name signs, the Board of Trade at the end of the start straight, the river below street level with the Riverwalk, bascule bridges with bridge houses and Wacker's balustrade, a flat paved city to the fog).
- **Crossover** (new): a 5-lap race against the 5 rivals on a short walled asphalt figure 8 whose 155 m straights cross at grade. Grippy asphalt, painted lines and kerbs, concrete walls you can scrape along (sparks, scratches) without crashing, gates past the crossing and on the loop apexes, a diamond over the next checkpoint instead of the bend arrow, gate posts behind the walls.
- **Destruction Bowl**: a 10-car derby in a mud bowl, last car running. Nine AI personalities (rammers, rear-enders, a spinner, a finisher, survivors, a glory hunter), takedowns, a health roster, spectating, sudden death, results.
- **Dune Loop**: the M3 race, unchanged apart from moving its world into `RaceDirector`.

**M3 is playable**:
- A two-lap race against 5 AI rivals on the Dune Loop, with positions, a minimap showing the opponents, and a results panel.
- The endless time trial is on T.
- About a third of the lap is open sand.
- Corner chevrons, chevron boards and jump stripes mark the hazards.
- Everything from M1 and M2 (damage, debris, FX, gates, stakes, parked cars, tuning panel) is unchanged.
- Crash balance: crashing alone is mild, car-car hits hurt, the faster car deals the damage, heavy/tough cars resist, and big hits knock cars off line (tail hits spin them). Knobs in Tuning → Damage.
- Repair stations at every checkpoint (dents and lost parts visibly restored). Rivals feud with each other and the player, attack in personality styles (PIT spins, shoves off the road), pit when damaged, get themselves unstuck, and are out when wrecked: no rescues.
